The 2024 Integrated Business Establishment Survey indicates that Ghana's business-establishment base grew from approximately 0.6 million in 2014 to 1.9 million in 2024, while persons engaged rose from 3.38 million to 6.90 million. Services now account for 76.6% of establishments.

For legal advisers, this translates into increasing demand for formalization, corporate structuring, governance, employment, tax, regulatory, data, finance, property and contractual support—which we provide to our clients.

Doing Business in Ghana: What Investors Should Understand

01

Choose the Right Vehicle

Consider whether the activity is best undertaken through a Ghanaian company, external company, partnership or another permitted structure.

02

Foreign Investment Status

Foreign participation can trigger investment-registration requirements and sector-specific rules. The investment framework is changing; investors should confirm the law and implementation position applicable at the time of entry.

03

Tax & Accounting

Businesses operating in Ghana generally need tax registration and ongoing filing and payment compliance with the Ghana Revenue Authority. The general corporate income tax rate is 25%.

04

Licences & Permits

Company incorporation does not by itself authorize every commercial activity. Regulated sectors may require approvals or licences from the relevant regulator.

05

People & Employment

Employers should plan for Ghanaian employment, payroll, workplace obligations, immigration and work permits for expatriate personnel where applicable.

06

Land, Premises & Assets

Property acquisition or leasing requires title, ownership, land-use, planning, environmental and transaction due diligence.
